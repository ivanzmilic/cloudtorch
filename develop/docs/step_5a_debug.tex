{\sffamily\bfseries\large\color{acc} cloudtorch / develop\, --- Step 5a debug: relaxing spatial regularisation}

\vspace{1pt}
{\sffamily\color{gry}\footnotesize 2026-08-24 --- fits from \co{step\_5a\_reg\_test.ipynb} (cell 20) were unsatisfactory. Hypothesis tested: the explicit \textbf{spatial} smoothness was over-constraining the cloud maps. Action: relax it $100\times$ and re-run to recover a good data fit before re-tightening.}

\vspace{1pt}{\footnotesize\color{gry}\rule{\textwidth}{0.4pt}}

%% ------------------------------------------------
\section{What was run}

The step-5a notebook config reproduced verbatim (given fixed background, \co{n\_bkg}=0; \co{CROP}=64, \co{N\_T}=8; $10^4$ cosine-annealed Adam iters), changing \emph{only} the spatial weight $w^{xy}\!:\,10^{-3}\!\to\!10^{-5}$ across all four cloud groups (\co{S},\co{dtau},\co{vlos},\co{dv}). Temporal $w^{t}$ and the soft hinges were left untouched.

%% ------------------------------------------------
\section{Result: essentially no change}

$\chi^2$ fell only because the penalty itself shrank; the \emph{data} fit did not improve. The parameter maps are unchanged: \co{vlos1} uniformly blue, \co{vlos2} uniformly red, neither crossing zero, both pinned within $\sim\!2$\,km/s of the $\mp20$ initialisation. The pixel spectra still miss the blue-wing asymmetry (pix 2575, 1608).

%% ------------------------------------------------
\section{Why relaxing reg could not help (the finding)}

There was no \emph{binding} spatial regularisation to relax. In both runs the total reg is only ${\sim}2.5\%$ of the loss ($0.011$ vs data $0.416$) and every hinge is inactive (velocities sit at $\mp22$, far inside the $\pm120$ range limit and below the $\Delta v\!=\!45$ cap). The fit is therefore \textbf{already effectively unregularised} --- so ``loosen reg, then tighten'' cannot get traction. The mediocre fit and the flat velocities are intrinsic to the \emph{setup}, not the penalties.

%% ------------------------------------------------
\section{Root cause of the velocity collapse}

The two clouds collapse to a symmetric $\mp22$\,km/s pair because (i) the \textbf{given background already owns the line wings}, where the Doppler signal lives, so the clouds get almost no velocity leverage; and (ii) the \textbf{symmetric $\mp20$ init} is a flat spot the data gradient never leaves --- line \emph{depth} (via $S,\Delta\tau$) dominates, velocity is second-order. So $S/\Delta\tau$ carry all the fitting (real fibril structure) while the Doppler DoF stay dead.

%% ------------------------------------------------
\section{Loss curve}

Converged by ${\sim}1$--$2$k iters (8.16 $\to$ ${\sim}0.42$), then flat for ${\sim}8$k --- the extra $90\%$ of the 711\,s run buys nothing (a degeneracy floor, not the noise floor: RMS $5\%$ is well above photon noise). Periodic order-of-magnitude spikes coincide with reg spikes: the autograd-smoothness double-backward occasionally emits a large gradient, Adam overshoots, then recovers.

%% ------------------------------------------------
\section{Next lever (not a reg knob)}

Attack the \textbf{velocity init / wing access}, one change at a time: \textbf{(1)} asymmetric \co{vlos} init (e.g. $-5$ / $+35$) or a per-pixel line-bisector warm-start, to escape the symmetric trap --- cheapest test; \textbf{(2)} if needed, let the clouds reach the wings the background currently claims. Only after the velocity field moves should the smoothness/hinge weights be revisited.
